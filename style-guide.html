The visual and verbal rulebook for Unfussy.Tech and its products. Use this as a reference when building new pages, writing copy, or creating marketing materials. Everything here is derived directly from style.css.

Usage rules: Amber (#EF9F27) is the primary brand colour — used for accents, CTAs, labels, links, and hover states. Dark (#1A1A18) is the primary text and dark-section background. Never use pure black. The warm off-white background (#FDFCF8) gives the site its distinctive warmth — do not substitute with pure white as a page background. Borders always use amber at low opacity (0.12–0.25) rather than grey.

Button rules: All buttons use border-radius: 100px (full pill). Never square or lightly rounded. Primary actions use amber fill. Secondary actions use ghost (dark border on light) or outline-light (white border on dark). All buttons lift 1px on hover (translateY(-1px)). Never use more than two buttons side by side.

10 — Voice & tone
The Unfussy voice in three words:
Honest
We name problems plainly. We admit what we don't do.
Warm
We're rooting for our customers. That shows in every sentence.
Dry wit
Never slapstick. A raised eyebrow, not a punchline.
✓ Do "Your spreadsheet is your CRM. It's just not very good at it."
✗ Don't "Supercharge your sales pipeline with our AI-powered CRM solution!"
✓ Do "We don't have a board reporting module. And we're proud of it."
✗ Don't "We're focused on providing core CRM functionality for SMBs."
✓ Do "No credit card. No onboarding call. No 47-step setup wizard."
✗ Don't "Getting started is quick and easy with our streamlined onboarding process!"
✓ Do "One price. Everything included. No surprises. Ever."
✗ Don't "Our transparent pricing model ensures you only pay for what you use."
Additional voice rules: Use em-dashes (—) for asides, not brackets. Short sentences land harder than long ones. It's okay to start a sentence with "And" or "But." Speak to "you" (the reader), not "businesses" or "teams." Call out the problem before the solution — empathy before pitch. Headings often break across two lines intentionally, with the second line carrying the emotional punch. Italic in Lora is used for the key phrase in a heading — never for decorative emphasis in body copy.
